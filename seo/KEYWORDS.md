# Keywords

Which searches to go after, in what order, and why the obvious ones are a trap.

---

## The mistake everyone makes first

The instinct is to target **"trading journal"**. It is the biggest term, so it
must be the best one.

It is the worst one to start with. Tradervue, TraderSync, Edgewonk and Tradezella
have spent years and real money on that phrase. A site with no domain history
does not enter that fight — not with better copy, not with perfect tags.

The move is to win searches they are not fighting for, get traffic and links
from those, and use the authority that builds to climb toward the big terms
later. This is not a consolation prize; it is the only route that has ever
worked for a new site.

### Why the long phrases are better anyway

| | "trading journal" | "how to stop revenge trading" |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Searches/month | high | low |
| Competitors | funded, established | blog posts and forum threads |
| Realistic time to rank | years | weeks to months |
| What the searcher wants | to compare products | to fix a problem **you fix** |
| Chance they sign up | low — they are shopping | high — they are hurting |

Someone searching "how to stop revenge trading" has just lost money doing it.
They are the best visitor RagDex can get, and nobody is competing hard for them.

## Tier 1 — write these first

Problem-led, low competition, and every one maps onto something RagDex already
does. These are article titles, near enough as-is.

| Target phrase | The article | What it leads to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| how to stop revenge trading | What revenge trading actually looks like in your data, and the rule that stops it | Behavioural leak detection |
| what to include in a trading journal | The twelve fields worth logging, and the four that are a waste of time | Quick Add |
| why win rate doesn't matter | A 42% win rate can be a great year. Here is the arithmetic. | Profit factor, expectancy |
| how to calculate profit factor | Profit factor, expectancy and R-multiple, worked through with real numbers | Journal summary cards |
| trading psychology journal template | Logging how you felt, and what to do with it afterwards | Emotion tracking |
| how to review your trading week | A weekly review that takes twenty minutes and actually changes behaviour | Analytics, AI coach |
| prop firm rule tracking | Staying inside daily loss limits when you are the one enforcing them | Settings → risk management |
| trading session times explained | Asia, London and New York, and why your results differ across them | Session filter |
| what is a rule adherence score | Grading your discipline separately from your P&L | Rule adherence scoring |
| trading journal for beginners | Your first month, and the three habits that outlast every strategy | The free plan |

**Why these and not others:** each is a real question, each has an answer RagDex
is unusually qualified to give, and none is dominated by a company with a
marketing budget. "why win rate doesn't matter" is the standout — it is
contrarian, it is correct, and contrarian-and-correct is what earns links.

## Tier 2 — after ten Tier 1 pages are live

Comparison and category terms. These convert well but need some authority first.

- trading journal with AI coach
- trading journal that tracks emotions
- free trading journal *(you have a free plan — say so)*
- trading journal for prop firm traders
- forex trading journal / crypto trading journal / futures trading journal
- `[competitor]` alternative — one page per competitor, honest about who each
  tool suits better. Honest comparisons rank and get linked to; dishonest ones
  get ignored and occasionally litigated.

## Tier 3 — the long game

- trading journal
- best trading journal
- trade tracking software

Do not aim here for at least a year. You will arrive via Tiers 1 and 2 or not
at all.

## What to do with each page

One page per phrase. Not one page mentioning all of them — that page ranks for
none of them, because it is not clearly about any one thing.

For each:

- Put the phrase in the `<title>`, the `<h1>`, and the first paragraph.
  Naturally. If it reads oddly out loud, it reads oddly to Google too.
- Answer the question in the first hundred words. People leave otherwise, and
  Google measures that.
- 1,200–2,000 words for a how-to. Long enough to be complete, not padded.
  Length is not a ranking factor; completeness is, and completeness takes room.
- Use real numbers from real trades. Specifics are what make a page the best
  answer rather than the tenth restatement of the same advice.
- Link to the relevant part of the app, once, where it genuinely helps.
- Link to your other articles. This is how Google learns which of your pages
  are important.
- Add `Article` structured data with a named author.

### The author byline is not optional here

Finance is what Google calls **Your Money or Your Life** — a category held to a
much higher standard, because bad advice does real damage. Anonymous content in
YMYL struggles no matter how good it is.

Put a real name on every article, with a short bio saying why that person knows
this. "Reuvin has been journaling his own trades since 2023 and built RagDex
after a spreadsheet stopped being enough" is worth more than any tag in this
repo. It is honest, it is specific, and it is exactly the signal Google is
looking for.

## Measuring it

In Search Console, **Performance** shows the queries you already appear for.

The highest-value report there: filter to **position 8–20**. Those are pages
Google already considers relevant but is not quite convinced by. Improving one
of them is far cheaper than creating a new page — often it is a better title, a
clearer opening paragraph, or two more internal links.

Check monthly, not daily. Nothing useful happens in a day.
